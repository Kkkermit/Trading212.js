Every order needs a key with the `orders:execute` permission. On live, the client must also be created with
`trading: "enabled"`; until then every order throws `TradingDisabledError` and nothing is sent.

| Method                                                | Does                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| `orders.buy(order, options?)`                         | Buys. The quantity is positive; `type` defaults to `market` |
| `orders.sell(order, options?)`                        | Sells. The quantity is positive and the sign is set for you |
| `orders.sellAll(ticker)`                              | Sells everything you can of one holding, at market          |
| `orders.market()`, `limit()`, `stop()`, `stopLimit()` | Send exactly what you give them; a negative quantity sells  |
| `orders.waitFor(order)`                               | Waits until an order is filled, cancelled or rejected       |
| `orders.list()`                                       | Orders that are still working                               |
| `orders.get(id)`                                      | One working order                                           |
| `orders.cancel(id)`                                   | Asks Trading 212 to cancel a working order                  |

Full signatures are in the [method reference](/Trading212.js/reference/methods/#orders).

## Place an order and wait for it

<Example name="place-and-wait" />

`buy()` and `sell()` take a **positive** quantity and set the direction themselves, so a stray minus sign cannot turn
a sale into a purchase. `waitFor()` polls the order once a second, its rate limit. Once the order stops being pending,
Trading 212 answers 404, so from then on `waitFor()` looks it up in the history instead, which needs the
`history:orders` permission. `timeoutMs` bounds the whole wait; after it, `waitFor()` throws `TimeoutError`, and the
order may still fill.

## Order types

<Example name="order-types" />

| Type        | Fills                                                    | Needs                                     | Limit       |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| `market`    | now, at the next price                                   | nothing else                              | 50 / minute |
| `limit`     | at `limitPrice` or better                                | `limitPrice`, `timeValidity`              | 1 / 2 s     |
| `stop`      | as a market order once the price reaches `stopPrice`     | `stopPrice`, `timeValidity`               | 1 / 2 s     |
| `stopLimit` | as a limit order at `limitPrice` once `stopPrice` is hit | `stopPrice`, `limitPrice`, `timeValidity` | 1 / 2 s     |

`timeValidity` is `"DAY"`, which expires at midnight in the exchange's time zone, or `"GOOD_TILL_CANCEL"`, which
stays until it fills or you cancel it. It is required on every non-market order, so an order never depends on a
default. `extendedHours: true` lets a market order fill outside regular hours, and is allowed on market orders only.

The lower-level `market()`, `limit()`, `stop()` and `stopLimit()` take the same fields without `type`, and send
exactly what you give them, with the API's own convention that **a negative quantity sells**:

```ts
await t212.orders.market({ ticker: "AAPL_US_EQ", quantity: -2 }); // sells 2
```

## What is checked before sending

Before any request, the client checks that:

- the ticker has no spaces;
- the quantity is a finite number, not zero, with at most **4 decimal places**;
- prices are positive;
- `timeValidity` is `"DAY"` or `"GOOD_TILL_CANCEL"`;
- no field is given that the order type does not use;
- the quantity is no more than the instrument's `maxOpenQuantity`, once this client has loaded the instrument list,
  for example through `instruments.resolve()`. The list is never downloaded for this, so without it Trading 212
  makes the call.

Every problem goes into one `ValidationError`, with `error.issues` listing them. Nothing is sent.

## Quantities

Orders take at most 4 decimal places; positions can hold 8. `truncateQuantity()` cuts towards zero using the number's
exact digits, never floating-point arithmetic, so it can never round up past what you own:

```ts
import { isValidQuantity, truncateQuantity } from "@kkkermit/trading212.js";

truncateQuantity(12.09032128); // 12.0903
isValidQuantity(0.12345); // false: 5 decimal places
```

The API does not support placing an order by value, such as "£50 of Apple".

## Sell a whole position

<Example name="sell-all" />

`sellAll()` reads the position's `quantityAvailableForTrading`, truncates it to 4 decimal places and sells that at
market. Shares in pies or reserved by pending orders are left alone. It needs the `portfolio` permission as well as
`orders:execute`.

## Preview with a dry run

<Example name="dry-run" />

A dry run checks the order and builds the exact request, then returns it instead of sending it. It works on a live
client with trading off, and `wouldSend` says whether this client would send it for real. TypeScript knows the
difference: `{ dryRun: true }` returns a `DryRunResult`, and anything else an `Order`.

## Working orders and cancelling

<Example name="cancel-pending" />

`cancelAll()` cancels every working order, or one ticker's, one request each, and returns a result per order:
`requested`, `gone` when it had already filled or been cancelled, `unknown` when the cancellation may or may not have
arrived, or `failed` with the error. One failure does not stop the rest; aborting its `signal` stops it before the
next order.

A successful `cancel()` means Trading 212 accepted the request; the order can still fill first. Cancelling an order
that has already gone throws `NotFoundError`. At most 50 orders may be pending per ticker.

To hear when orders fill or are cancelled without asking yourself, use a
[watcher](/Trading212.js/guides/watching/).

## When an order fails

| Error                          | Means                                                                               |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `ValidationError`              | Refused before sending; fix what `error.issues` lists                               |
| `OrderRejectedError`           | Refused by Trading 212; `error.reason` is its code, such as `min-quantity-exceeded` |
| `TradingDisabledError`         | Trading is off for this client, as it is on live by default; nothing was sent       |
| `OrderOutcomeUnknownError`     | It may have gone through. `orders.findAfterUnknown(error)` looks for it             |
| `NetworkError` (`sent: false`) | It never left your machine, so it is safe to send again                             |
| `MissingScopeError`            | The key lacks `orders:execute`                                                      |

<Aside type="caution" title="Never resend blindly">
	An order is never retried automatically. When the outcome is unknown, look before you send it again; the [safe retry
	recipe](/Trading212.js/recipes/#resend-an-order-safely) shows how.
</Aside>

## Finding an order whose outcome is unknown

<Example name="safe-retry" />

`findAfterUnknown(error)` takes the `OrderOutcomeUnknownError` and looks for the order among the working orders and
the recent history, matching its ticker, direction, type, quantity and prices, and only orders created within five
minutes of `error.sentAt` (change it with `windowMs`). It never sends anything, so whether to place the order again
stays your decision:

| `status`    | Means                                                                                     |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `found`     | It reached Trading 212: do not send it again. Check `order.status`, as it may be rejected |
| `not-found` | Nothing like it is working or in the recent history                                       |
| `unclear`   | Several orders match, one is like it but differs, or the history is too busy to search    |

An order that has the same ticker, direction and time but differs in a detail, such as its quantity or where it was
placed from, makes the answer `unclear` rather than `not-found`, so a detail Trading 212 lists differently can never
make an order that went through look as if it did not. When the answer is `unclear`, check the app before sending
anything. A new order can take a moment to appear in Trading 212's lists, so wait a few seconds before deciding it was not
placed. It needs `orders:read` and `history:orders`.

A refusal says in plain words what the order broke, such as
`a quantity of 0.1 is below the smallest Trading 212 accepts for this order, 1.33914872.`, and carries the limit as a
number: `error.minQuantity`, `error.maxQuantity`, `error.maxDecimals` or `error.ownedQuantity`. Too small, too large,
too many decimal places, selling more than you own and not enough cash have all been seen on demo; a closed market is
recognised by its expected code. The [errors guide](/Trading212.js/guides/errors/#orderrejectederror) has the full
table.
